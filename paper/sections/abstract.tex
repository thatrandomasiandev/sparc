Robots that learn from human preference comparisons ask people which short
behaviors look better in order to infer a reward function. Much of the
uncertainty about that reward never changes what the robot should actually do,
so limited human patience is spent resolving differences with no behavioral
consequence.

The missing piece is a practical way to choose trajectory comparisons for the
information that reduces policy regret, using a prior fit to previous users
rather than searching all of reward space.
% C-mech-2: require E-prior before ``population prior makes \ldots usable'' stays.
We select comparisons by expected reduction in policy regret under a
population prior and evaluate query-selection strategies on a tabular
gridworld with exact optimal policies and exact regret, using simulated users
drawn from a mixture preference population.

A preliminary study finds a large gap between regret-based (and BALD) acquisition
and a true-regret oracle, indicating substantial headroom beyond one-step VOI
variants we tried (particle loss, lookahead).

Whether regret-based selection reliably beats parameter-uncertainty acquisition
is reserved for a powered paired evaluation (see Experiments).
These results motivate shifting preference elicitation toward what changes robot
behavior, while insisting that every reported number trace to a named experiment.
