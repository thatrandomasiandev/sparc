\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Robots learn reward models from human comparisons of trajectory segments.
Because each comparison costs scarce human attention, query selection---which pair to
ask next---is the practical budget, not compute.
The dominant practice maximizes information about reward parameters
\citep{sadigh2017active,biyik2019asking}.

That target is misaligned with how a robot acts.
Information-gain and BALD criteria~\citep{houlsby2011bald} shrink uncertainty over
parameters; the robot then deploys a \emph{policy} induced by its reward estimate.
Prior work already argued that optimizing parameter uncertainty can be wasteful:
Model-Free Preference Elicitation~\citep{modelfree2024} moved the elicitation target
from parameters to reward equivalence classes and recommendation-quality value of
information.
Independently, \citet{regan2009regret} developed regret-based reward elicitation for
MDPs, selecting queries to reduce decision-relevant uncertainty about near-optimal
policies.
We instantiate that decision-theoretic goal for preference-based robotics: score
queries by expected reduction in \emph{policy regret} under a \emph{population particle
prior} over previous users, rather than searching unrestricted reward space.
On our domain, nearby unit rewards can leave the optimal policy unchanged in a local
neighborhood at low feature dimension; that local stability collapses as dimension
grows---motivating a regret target, not a claim that irrelevance expands with~$d$.

We study binary segment comparisons under Bradley--Terry likelihoods and linear state
rewards. For a fixed policy, value is linear in the weight vector, so batch policy
regret is exact and cheap.
The loop is select (VOI on regret), update the posterior, then decide (act).
We evaluate against random sampling, BALD, and a true-regret oracle; we do not claim a
new elicitation paradigm, only this instantiation.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Formalize preference-query acquisition as expected policy-regret reduction under a
        population particle prior, using linearity of policy value to compute exact batch
        regret for fixed policies.
  \item Pre-register a paired comparison of random, BALD, particle VOI, and a true-regret
        oracle under exact policy regret, reporting interval estimates and the remaining
        oracle gap rather than a bake-off win.
  \item Report diagnostics that bound interpretation where evidence exists: a
        particle-coverage diagnostic for residual regret (no calibrated threshold
        claimed here), policy-equivalence measurement versus dimension, and
        consistency-identification negatives (factored consistency latents and
        difficulty regimes).
\end{enumerate}
