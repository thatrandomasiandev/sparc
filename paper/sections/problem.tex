\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Reward model}
\label{sec:reward}
A user is characterized by preference weights $w\in\mathbb{R}^d$ and consistency
$b>0$.
States carry features $\phi(s)\in\mathbb{R}^d$; the linear reward is
$r(s)=w\cdot\phi(s)$.
Under Bradley--Terry, answer probabilities depend on the product $b\|w\|$ only, so
only that scale is identifiable: we pin $\|w\|=1$ and put all scale into $b$.
A trajectory segment is summarized by discounted feature counts
\begin{equation}
  f=\sum_{t}\gamma^{t}\,\phi(s_{t}).
\end{equation}
Our tabular elicitation experiments use single-state segments (segment horizon
$H{=}1$), so $f=\phi(s)$ there; we do not treat those runs as multi-step
discounted occupancy counts.

\subsection{Response model}
\label{sec:response}
Queries are binary comparisons of segments $A$ and $B$.
With $\|w\|=1$,
\begin{equation}
  P(A\succ B)=\sigma\bigl(b\,w\cdot(f_A-f_B)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the logistic sigmoid.\footnote{%
Full rankings follow Plackett--Luce; Bradley--Terry is the $K{=}2$ special case.
Main experiments use binary comparisons.}

\subsection{Population setting}
\label{sec:population}
Previous users $u_1,\ldots,u_N$ each provide a small number of comparisons,
inducing a \emph{population prior} over $(w,b)$.
A new user arrives with unknown $(w,b)$.
The robot asks $T$ preference queries, then acts by deploying a policy on the MDP.
We maintain a particle belief drawn from that prior; acquisition and the
select--update--decide loop are deferred to~\cref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Objective}
\label{sec:objective}
Let $\rho$ denote the robot's belief (or the decision induced by it), and let
$\pi(\rho)$ be the policy executed after deciding from $\rho$.
Under the true reward $w$, policy regret is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Reg}(\rho)=V^{*}_{w}-V^{\pi(\rho)}_{w},
\end{equation}
the gap between optimal value and the value of the executed policy
(cf.\ regret-based elicitation for MDPs~\citep{regan2009regret}; we do not claim
the criterion as new).
Because the value of a fixed policy is linear in $w$ for tabular linear rewards,
this regret is exact and cheap to evaluate in batch.
We take \emph{low-budget} mean regret after $t\le 3$ queries as the
\textbf{primary}, pre-registered evaluation target; final-query regret is
\textbf{secondary}.
No empiric comparisons are reported in this section.

\subsection{Oracle}
\label{sec:oracle}
As an evaluation ceiling we include a true-regret \emph{oracle}: an acquisition
that scores candidates using ground-truth regret under the known $(w,b)$.
It is never a baseline to beat; it appears in every elicitation table
(\cref{sec:experiments}) to bound the remaining gap under the same candidate pool
and decision rule.
